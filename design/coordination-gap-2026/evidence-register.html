Replace

"16% top Illinois rate" is the weakest true version of your best number

A percentage nobody can convert into dollars is doing almost none of the work available here. The Illinois Attorney General publishes computation examples in its own fact sheet: an estate of $4,000,000 owes $0; an estate of $5,000,000 owes $285,714, both on all-Illinois property, both with zero federal tax. That is 28.6 percent of the first million above the line, it is dollar-denominated, and it comes from the office that collects the tax. Lead with it and retire the 16 percent as a footnote.

Fix

The ten-year clock, as printed, is broader than the regulation

The current sheet says IRS enforcement of annual distributions began in 2025 without qualification. Annual distributions in years one through nine are required only where the account owner had already begun required minimum distributions before death. Where the owner died before their required beginning date, the heir has the ten-year deadline with no annual requirement. The 25 percent excise tax is also reducible to 10 percent if the shortfall is corrected within a two-year window. This is exactly the kind of over-broad statement Louis Wierenga's CPA counterpart catches, and the corrected version is on the rebuilt sheet.

Section 02

How the ranking works

Each item is scored one to five on four axes and totalled out of twenty. The axes are deliberately weighted toward a stranger's living room, not a conference agenda.

Felt

Does a person with no financial literacy feel it in the first reading? Dollars beat percentages. Local beats national.

Decision

Is it attached to something the household or the professional must actually do, ideally with a deadline on it?

Defensible

Does it survive a hostile CPA? Statute and regulator publications score five. Asset-manager marketing scores two.

Only you

Does it argue for Driftwood, or does it argue for hiring any advisor at all? The second kind is worth very little.

The pattern the scoring exposes: every item in the top tier is a fact about the tax code or the state statute. Not one of them is a study about the value of advice. The industry numbers that look most flattering, Russell's 4.92 percent and Vanguard's Advisor's Alpha, score at the bottom, because every competitor in the corridor can cite them and none of them are attached to a decision.
