## 2. The actual ceiling of each paper

### Paper 1 — *From Pitcher Fatigue to Market Efficiency*

**Core contribution.** A well-powered null: across 163 games, no publicly observable baseball state
variable improves on a sharp live market's own forecast of remaining runs (out-of-sample R² −0.037,
Clark–West does not favour the augmented model). Plus two things that outlive the null — a clean
demonstration that the one variable which *appears* to beat the market is post-treatment selection
bias, and the Third Turn Protocol as a reusable validation ladder.

**Novelty: moderate.** The null is consistent with a large prior literature; what is fresh is the
setting (live, in-play, event-dated) and the framing (a candidate must beat an incumbent forecast,
not merely predict the outcome). The post-treatment-bias demonstration is the most novel single
element and is the part most likely to be cited on its own.

**Identification credibility: high.** Leave-one-game-out out-of-sample evaluation, a nested-model
test appropriate to the comparison, an explicit power statement bounding what the design can rule
out, and an inference discussion that brackets the effective sample between observations and games
rather than picking the flattering end.

**Strongest feature.** Not the result — the *discipline*. A null reported with a power analysis, a
self-refuted candidate variable, and byte-identical reproduction from committed inputs. At a journal
with a reproducibility check this is a genuine competitive advantage, and very few submissions can
match it.

**Biggest limitation.** Scale. 163 games, one month, one sport, one book. The boundary is
characterized precisely but narrowly, and a referee at a top outlet will ask why a single month
should generalize. The paper concedes this in §6 rather than hiding it, which helps, but it does not
make the sample larger.

**Editorial quality: high.** Clean, unhedged prose; limitations stated without editorializing; no
overclaiming in the abstract.

**Does it justify a shot at a genuinely high-prestige outlet? Yes — one.** Not top-5 economics and
not top-3 finance; a 163-game null in one sport will not clear those bars, and submitting there
would burn a year to learn nothing. But Management Science has published this literature since the
racetrack-betting era, and the paper's methodological framing plus its reproducibility standard make
it a defensible, non-delusional attempt. Take the shot once, with a good cover letter, and move down
the ladder quickly on rejection.

### Paper 2 — *What Prices Cannot Tell You*

**Core contribution.** A pre-registered gate, applied literally to the evidence, returned
non-identification — and the paper reports that instead of retreating to a weaker estimate it could
have published. The general claim is portable: timestamps are observations, latency is an inference,
and any transmission estimate is a joint statement about the market and the apparatus that observed
it.

**Novelty: high, and higher than Paper 1's.** Two elements are genuinely uncommon. First, a paper
that pre-commits to conditions and then honours them against its own interest, with the amendment
disclosed as post-evidence and a tripwire attached. Second, treating the *delivery path* — cache
policy, delivered-object staleness, out-of-order arrival — as market microstructure rather than as
plumbing to be assumed away. The second is close to unoccupied.

**Identification credibility: very high, precisely because of what it refuses to claim.** The paper's
authority comes from the fact that it declines the estimate. The four latencies are held apart
throughout; every statistic reported concerns delivery; nothing bounds publication latency, and the
paper says so.

**Strongest feature.** The governance structure is itself a citable methodological object. The gate,
the outcome taxonomy, the disclosed amendment, the tripwire — this is a template other empirical
work can adopt, and it is the element most likely to be cited outside sports betting entirely.

**Biggest limitation.** It is a negative result about a negative result, and some editors will read
"no estimate" as "no paper." Beyond framing: two books, one of which barely quotes; Condition 1's
extraction non-invariance is deferred rather than cured; and one robustness question (truncated
matchups on per-matchup interval statistics) is stated as open.

**Editorial quality: very high.** The best-written object in the program. §9 in particular earns the
generalization rather than asserting it.

**Does it justify a shot at a genuinely high-prestige outlet? Yes — and it is the better candidate
of the two.** Its contribution is methodological and portable, which is what high-prestige
methodology outlets reward. The risk is not quality; it is that the paper gets desk-rejected for
having no positive finding. That risk is managed by venue choice and framing, not by weakening the
paper.

**Two policy facts that change the calculus.** IJF's mandatory data-and-code regime with a
reproducibility check is normally a burden; for this paper it is an *advantage*, because it

Science's Finance department treats rejected submissions as fault-free — a rejected paper may return
once as a fresh submission — which materially lowers the cost of taking the stretch shot first.

**Ladder: Management Science → IJF → Journal of Sports Economics → JQAS.**
Cap the stretch at one round. If Management Science desk-rejects, do not appeal and do not try a
second top-tier finance outlet; go straight to IJF, where the paper's reproducibility is worth real
points.

**On the measurement conferences.** Both 2027 CFPs are unannounced; the dates above are projections
from the prior cycle, not facts. More importantly, both demand the substantial rewrite the program

JFEC is the highest-probability serious placement and the one where the paper reads as *in scope*
rather than as an outsider. Take one JBES round first anyway: the downside is a few months, and the
upside is a substantially better-placed methodological contribution.

**Framing note that matters more than venue choice.** Lead with the identification result, not with
the sportsbook. A cover letter and abstract that open on "when can a measured interval be attributed
to one stage of an unobserved process?" will survive editorial screening that "we could not measure
which sportsbook is faster" will not.

## 4. Supplements

### Paper 1 — no supplement should be submitted

`docs/VISUAL_COMPANION.md` **does not materially strengthen the paper and must not accompany it.**
Its own opening states that it "is not part of the paper," "carries no citable claim," and uses
"small buckets and wide intervals… meant to build intuition, not to support inference." Submitting a
document that disclaims its own inferential content invites exactly the wrong question.

It is nonetheless a genuinely good artifact — it is the single best *outreach* object in the
program, and §5 puts it to work there.

**Should anything move between main text and supplement? No.** Appendices A (candidate hypotheses),
B (construction, power, forecast-error level) and C (transaction costs) are load-bearing for a null
result: a referee assessing whether the study could have detected an effect will want the power
analysis adjacent, not in a separate file. Moving them out would weaken the paper. Keep all three in
the main text.

**Is it publication-ready?** As outreach, yes — and it now has a committed builder, which it lacked.
As a journal supplement, no, and it should not be made into one.

### Paper 2 — a supplement is warranted, and is built

A referee's first question about a pre-registered gate is whether the rule really was fixed in
advance or arrived at afterwards. That question cannot be answered inside a manuscript without
derailing it, and it is fatal if left unanswered. `paper2_supplement.pdf` (5pp) answers it from the
record:

- **§S2** the four conditions and each disposition, including why Condition 1 fails on substance
  rather than on reproducibility — the distinction most likely to be misread;
- **§S3** the post-evidence amendment, its scoping-not-satisfying character, and the tripwire;
- **§S4** the delivery-path measurements, and the four latencies held apart;
- **§S5** the DROP-SLICE / DROP-MATCHUP robustness table, with the one open question stated;
- **§S6** the three continuity gaps, including the observation that two were invisible to a monitor
  reporting healthy — structurally the same error as trusting a delivery timestamp;
- **§S7** the as-of-instant reproducibility standard and what ships versus what does not.

Every figure and disposition in it is drawn from existing committed material. No new number appears.

## 5. Dissemination

The failure mode to avoid is not rejection. It is two good papers that nobody reads.

### Before submission

1. **Post Paper 1 and Paper 2 to SSRN** (Financial Economics Network) and **arXiv**
   (`q-fin.TR`/`econ.EM` for Paper 1; `cs.NI`/`econ.EM` for Paper 2). Both are permitted by every
   venue on the ladder. SSRN is where this literature's readership actually is; arXiv is where the
   measurement audience is. Post Paper 2's supplement alongside it.
2. **Register in RePEc** via the SSRN listing so the work enters economists' default discovery path.
3. **Publication repo**: re-sync `alecmessino/third-turn` to carry the final PDFs and the Paper 2
   supplement, and create the `v1.0` tag at `d709878`. Paper 1's own citation depends on that tag.
4. **Do not broadcast on social media** while a double-blind submission is live. Every venue on the
   ladder permits the preprint; IMC specifically asks authors not to push it where reviewers will
   trip over it. Quiet posting, no amplification.

### During review

5. **Targeted, non-public outreach.** Send the preprint directly to the handful of people who work
   on in-play market efficiency and on measurement-limit results. Ten well-chosen emails outperform
   any amount of posting.
6. **Talks, not press.** Seminars and a workshop presentation are legitimate under every anonymity
   policy on the ladder and are the highest-yield activity available during review.
7. **Publish the visual companion** as the public-facing explainer, clearly labelled as
   non-citable. It is the artifact a non-specialist can actually read.
8. **Let the Protocol travel on its own.** It is domain-general, wholly owned, and does not depend
   on either paper's fate. A short standalone write-up aimed at forecasting practitioners is the
   cheapest citation-generating act available.

### After acceptance

9. **Mint the DOI** — after, and only after, R1–R7 (§6). Deposit Tier A immediately; Tier B only if
   cleared.
10. **Update the preprints** with the published DOI, as JFEC and others require.
11. **Write the plain-language summary** — one page, no equations, aimed at practitioners and
    journalists. Paper 2's thesis ("the timestamp tells you when the object arrived, not when the
    price changed") is unusually explainable and is the most likely thing to travel beyond academia.

## 6. Data rights and reproducibility — kept separate

**Academic validity does not depend on redistribution rights, and must not be held hostage to them.**

### Can accompany submission *now*

- Both manuscripts, the supplement, all figures, all anonymized copies.
- All analysis and figure code (MIT).
- The frozen derived artifacts `output/*.json` — Tier A, sufficient to reproduce every number in
  Paper 1.
- `requirements-lock.txt`, `paper/REPRODUCE.md`, `benchmark/dataset/panels_schema.md`.
- The governance record, including the gate determination Paper 2 cites by path.
- A truthful availability statement. Paper 2's is already written; Paper 1's needs the same
  treatment if a journal challenges the benchmark-dataset claim.

### Must wait for R1–R7 / legal / ToS review

- Any Zenodo deposit or DOI covering Tier B (`data/trajectories.jsonl`, `closing_lines.csv`) or
  Tier C (the raw panels).
- Any relicensing of collected data under CC BY or equivalent.
- Any commercial use or redistribution of the collected quotes.

### The one place these genuinely collide

IJF requires data and code sharing. Its policy provides for exemption on motivated request to the
Editor-in-Chief, and unresolved third-party terms is a textbook motivation. The paper reproduces
byte-identically from the derived caches without the restricted data, which is a far stronger
position than most exemption requests can claim. **Draft that exemption paragraph before
submitting, not after an editor asks.** The same applies to JFEC's replication requirement.

**Nothing above blocks submission of either paper today.**
