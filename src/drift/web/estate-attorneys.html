For estate counsel

Your documents only work if the assets agree with them.

Driftwood does not draft, amend, or interpret. It keeps titling, beneficiaries, and funding consistent with what you drafted, holds the open decisions in one register, and brings you in before a decision moves rather than after it has.

The seats

Three seats, and only one of them is ours.

You are a permanent seat, not a relationship being routed around. Driftwood holds the seat no one owns today: keeping the balance sheet consistent with the instruments.

You
The instruments. Drafting, amendment, interpretation, and the legal judgment behind them. Driftwood provides no legal advice and holds no view on your drafting.
Driftwood
The register. Titling, beneficiary designations, funding status, and every open decision that touches them, each with an owner and the date it is next examined.
The household
The decision. Nothing is presented to them as a document instruction. Where the call is legal, it goes to you first, with the financial picture attached.

Driftwood coordinates with your firm and does not provide legal advice. Your engagement, and your client relationship, remain yours.

The risk it removes

The three ways good drafting quietly stops working.

The trust is signed, and never funded.

Funding is a standing register entry with a date, not a closing item. It stays open, and visible, until every asset is actually retitled.

A beneficiary form quietly overrides the plan.

Every designation is read against the documents at onboarding, and re-read whenever an account is opened, rolled, or inherited.

You hear about the sale after the letter of intent.

You are brought in while the entity, the timing, and the gifting capacity are still choices, not once value is locked in.

Where the seats move together

The moments the documents and the balance sheet have to agree.

A new or amended trust A funding schedule and the current titling of every account, in one page The instrument is funded as drafted, on a date, rather than eventually
