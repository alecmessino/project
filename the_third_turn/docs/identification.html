What prices cannot tell you

A market that has already priced everything public must have priced it somehow. This page lets you try to recover that mechanism from the data an outside observer actually holds, and watch yourself fail.

Companion to Identifying Information Transmission in Live Markets. Every control below is live. Nothing here reports an estimated result.

1. The equivalence you cannot escape

A run scores. Two sportsbooks re-price. We record one timestamp from each. The interval we record is a sum: how long the book took to decide, plus how long its feed took to publish. Only the first is economics.

Markets reveal prices. They do not reveal how those prices came to be.

This is not a power problem. Collecting a thousand more games moves every configuration above by exactly nothing, because the ambiguity is in the mapping from mechanism to observation rather than in the sample. Separating the two requires information from outside the timing series: an independent measurement of feed transport, or a defensible argument that transport is common to both books.

2. The instrument has a floor

The second constraint is our own. We poll the books on a fixed schedule, so everything that happens between two polls arrives as one observation. Drag the interval and watch the game blur.

3. What each rung would buy

Identification is not computed, it is assumed and then defended. These are the assumptions in order of dependence: each rung needs the one above it.

  1. Observed timestampswe hold these have
  2. A common event clockevent and quote times comparable auditable
  3. One well defined price seriesbooks post several lines at once bounded
  4. Feed latency known or common modenot measurable from a public endpoint open
  5. Pricing latency identifiedthe quantity we actually want blocked

The fourth rung is the one that decides the paper. Where exchange side timestamps and order identifiers exist, it is satisfied by construction and none of this arises. Where they do not, an estimate that would be unremarkable elsewhere becomes an inference resting on an unstated assumption about publishing infrastructure.

Observation survives. Identification does not.
